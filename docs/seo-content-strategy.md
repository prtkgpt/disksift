# DiskSift consumer SEO strategy

## Audience

DiskSift's primary search audience is an everyday Mac owner who has run out of space and is worried about deleting the wrong thing. Developer cleanup remains a product capability, but it is not the editorial center of gravity.

## Search jobs

1. **Urgent problem:** “Mac storage full,” “startup disk full,” “MacBook Air storage full.”
2. **Confusing diagnosis:** “System Data too large,” “Documents taking up space,” “storage not updating.”
3. **Safe action:** “what can I safely delete,” “free space without deleting important files.”
4. **Owned-app cleanup:** Photos, Messages attachments, Mail attachments, and iPhone backups.
5. **Tool evaluation:** “disk space analyzer Mac,” “safe Mac cleaner,” and “Mac cleaner without subscription.”

## Editorial rules

- Answer the query directly in the first paragraph.
- Use plain English and visible Mac menu paths.
- Separate replaceable files from personal or system data.
- Prefer Apple Support as the primary source for macOS behavior.
- Never imply that old, large, or unfamiliar automatically means safe to delete.
- Include a relevant next guide and the sitewide free-download CTA.
- Publish only complete articles; keep additional articles in REVIEW until checked.
- Use honest publication dates. Do not backdate new content.
- Keep developer articles available in the CMS, but out of the primary publication queue.

## Review queue

- Mail attachments
- Large videos
- Screenshots
- Cloud files using local space
- Mac cleaners without subscriptions
- Disk analyzer vs cleaner
- Empty Trash not freeing space

## Measurement

Track impressions, clicks, query, country, device, and average position in Google Search Console. Track blog CTA clicks and downloads in first-party application metrics or existing analytics. Review pages after 30–45 days; consolidate overlapping pages instead of creating another near-duplicate article.
